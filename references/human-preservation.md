# Human Preservation and Style Integration

Read this whenever an Edit Target contains a visible person. The objective is a more authored finished frame without changing who the person is, how their face is constructed, or how their body is posed.

Human preservation and painterly integration are separate requirements. Preserve the person as a hard geometric and semantic anchor; repaint their visible surface so they belong to the same light, colour, edge and material system as the transformed environment.

## Human Hard Lock

Record and preserve all visible, recognition-critical human evidence:

- identity, count, age presentation and expression;
- head silhouette, hair silhouette, head tilt and head-to-neck attachment;
- eye line, gaze, eye spacing and tilt, brow relationship, nose–mouth–chin axis, jaw and cheek proportion;
- body proportion, torso direction, shoulder/hip relationship and centre of gravity;
- pose, gesture, limb count, limb length, joint position and joint angle;
- visible hands, finger grouping, feet, shoe orientation and ground/contact relationship;
- clothing category, silhouette, major openings, protected patterns and identity-relevant local colours;
- ownership/contact relations between the person and a prop, seat, ground, another person or environment.

Do not locally enlarge the head, eyes, hands, legs or other body parts; change facial ratios; alter gaze or expression; straighten or bend limbs; invent or remove fingers; change weight distribution; or redraw clothing in a way that changes the body underneath.

The whole person may acquire a different apparent scale only through an explicitly authorised crop/restage or a uniform undistorted whole-person transform. Internal proportions, pose, anatomy, contact and silhouette relationships remain unchanged. Never use a face, hand or limb as a scale-takeover or perspective-distortion device in a human-preserving edit.

If exact source pixels are required, keep the person outside generative redraw and use deterministic masking/compositing. Do not promise exact pixels from a generative edit.

## What May Be Stylised

Stylisation may change the rendering language while keeping the human lock:

- group existing light and shadow into connected planes without moving facial landmarks or body contours;
- simplify skin texture, hair strands, minor clothing wrinkles and incidental hardware;
- change brush direction, paint-edge character, microcontrast and material marks inside the locked silhouette;
- let a motivated scene light and limited reflected colour affect reachable planes without replacing protected local colour;
- adjust edge hierarchy so the eyes, expression, gesture and contact points remain readable;
- reduce or strengthen detail only to match the intended viewing scale and the rest of the frame.

“More beautiful” means clearer light, cleaner edge relationships, coherent planes and better scene integration of the same person. It never authorises a new face, cosmetic-template proportions, anatomy repair by invention, or a different pose.

## Environment and Composition Freedom

After the human lock is recorded, the surrounding design may be authored more freely when the user allows it:

- crop around the protected person, redistribute headroom and negative space, or change the apparent whole-person scale uniformly;
- enlarge or simplify a landform, prop, architecture, weather mass, shadow, reflection or light shape;
- shift horizon, depth interval, repeated-detail positions or background area only when protected contact/topology still makes sense;
- introduce or enlarge a source-owned foreground occluder that does not cover recognition-critical facial, hand, limb or contact evidence;
- merge incidental trees, windows, grass, rocks, clouds, lights or foliage into broader rhythm groups;
- rebalance or re-script environment colour while keeping protected skin, hair, wardrobe and prop colour families stable.

Choose one primary directorial departure and one supporting move. In a human-preserving edit, the primary change should usually operate on crop/area, whole-person-to-environment scale, environment form, negative space, perspective/depth, light shape or colour-zone adjacency—not on human anatomy.

## Style Integration Contract

The protected person must not look pasted, photographic, over-sharp or generated by a different visual system. Require all of the following:

1. **Shared light field:** person and environment agree on key direction, shadow family, limited bounce and atmospheric falloff.
2. **Shared colour logic:** protected local colours remain recognisable while scene illumination, reflected colour and final grade affect person and environment coherently.
3. **Shared abstraction family:** the person uses finer connected planes than the background when needed, but both share compatible shape simplification and brush rhythm.
4. **Shared edge hierarchy:** facial and gesture edges may be the sharpest, while hair, clothing and body boundaries selectively merge, break or soften into the same atmosphere as nearby forms.
5. **Shared material world:** skin, hair, cloth, shoes and props have distinct grammars, yet their mark scale, paint body and surface response belong to the same finished painting as foliage, snow, water, buildings or sky.
6. **Contact integration:** feet, seat, shadow, held prop, overlapping garment and nearby surfaces share believable contact shadow, reflected colour and overlap; no cutout halo or disconnected outline.
7. **Unified final grade:** the subject is not separately brightened, sharpened, saturated, smoothed or rim-lit to force separation.

Judge the completed image, not whether the prompt contains preservation words. A geometrically correct person still fails when they look pasted into a differently rendered environment; a stylistically integrated person fails when the face or limbs drift.

## Prompt Contract

```text
Human hard lock: preserve the same person, identity, expression, head/hair silhouette, facial ratios and landmarks, gaze, head/neck attachment, body proportions, pose, limb count/length/joint angles, hands/feet, clothing silhouette and person–prop/ground contacts. Do not redesign anatomy, face or gesture. Any apparent scale change must be a uniform whole-person result of the authorised crop/restage.

Style integration: repaint the locked person and the transformed environment inside one shared key light, shadow family, reflected-colour system, final grade, edge hierarchy and compatible painterly abstraction. Keep facial planes finer and clearer where needed, but avoid a photographic cutout, separate sharpening, halo, generic beautification or different texture language. Preserve human geometry while allowing the surrounding crop, negative space, environment masses, light shape and colour zones to carry the primary directorial change.
```

## Human Verification Gate

Inspect source and output side by side at close scale and mid scale.

Fail immediately if any applicable item drifts without explicit permission:

- identity, expression, gaze or head tilt;
- eye line/spacing/tilt, nose–mouth–chin axis, jaw/cheek proportion or head-to-neck attachment;
- body proportion, shoulder/hip relationship, centre of gravity or pose;
- limb count, length, joint position/angle, hand/finger grouping, foot/shoe orientation or contact;
- clothing silhouette/openings, protected pattern/colour or prop ownership/contact;
- person–environment integration through light, colour, edge, material or contact.

If human geometry fails, make one targeted human restoration while keeping the accepted environment, composition, colour roles and primary directorial change fixed. If geometry passes but the person looks pasted, correct only shared light, reflected colour, contact, edge and material integration. Reinspect after the correction. If either problem remains, disclose the limitation and do not present the output as a pass.

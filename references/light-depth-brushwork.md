# Light Tactile Depth

Read this only when the user asks for visible oil-paint dimensionality or when an output must be reviewed for mosaic, patchwork or uniform-relief failures.

## Purpose

Use slight paint body to make existing spatial relationships easier to feel. Composition, value, colour, shape, overlap, perspective, scale falloff and edge hierarchy must already create the scene. Tactile depth is a final supporting cue, not the material from which the entire image is assembled.

The desired read is a painted image with occasional physical presence—not a relief sculpture, bump-mapped canvas, tiled palette-knife study or wall of separate paint chips.

## Depth Belongs to Relationships

A raised or loaded stroke is justified when it clarifies one of these relationships:

- a nearer form crosses or occludes a farther field;
- a contour turns toward or away from the viewer;
- two forms meet through contact, compression, attachment or support;
- a fold, ridge, wave, snow lip, foliage cluster or another directional form crests in space;
- a focal prop, garment edge, structure or foreground element separates from what sits behind it;
- one material transition needs a small tactile boundary to preserve depth or recognition.

These are categories, not a checklist. Use only those visible in the current source. If the same depth would read without a raised mark, the passage may remain flat.

## Keep Continuous Fields Continuous

Broad surfaces and atmospheres should read as connected shapes before individual marks. Usually keep these comparatively low-relief unless the source gives a specific local reason:

- sky, haze, fog and distant atmosphere;
- broad skin planes and identity-critical facial regions;
- calm water, snow, sand, walls and large fabric fields;
- distant vegetation, architecture and terrain;
- broad shadow or colour fields that provide quiet space.

Low-relief does not mean smooth, photographic or empty. Brush direction, broken colour, dry edges and planar shifts may remain visible. What must disappear is equal-height tessellation that turns a continuous form into a puzzle.

## Spatial Falloff

Let tactile prominence usually decrease with distance and contextual importance. Foreground overlaps and a few focal transitions may carry slightly more body; supporting forms use quieter broken paint; distant fields rely mainly on value, hue, scale and atmospheric edges.

Do not turn this into a numeric height ladder. The useful rule is perceptual: the surface must not flatten spatial hierarchy by giving every region equal physical presence.

## Stroke and Surface Behaviour

- Follow the depicted form, material flow, gravity or gesture.
- Prefer irregular connected passages over repeated equal-size rectangles.
- Let loaded body taper, merge, scrape or settle into surrounding paint instead of ending as isolated tiles.
- Keep plane breaks related to form and light; faceted modelling is not a licence for polygonal relief.
- Use palette-knife pressure selectively for a structural lip, compressed plane or scrape-back—not as a full-frame stamp.
- Keep grazing catches narrow, broken and subordinate. They describe a few compatible ridge crests, not the represented object's entire highlight system.
- Preserve chromatic pigment body. Thickness does not require white mixing, gray troughs or increased saturation.

## Identity and Beauty Boundary

Keep faces, skin, hands, text, product edges, garment openings, footwear junctions and other recognition-critical boundaries gentle. A tactile accent must not change anatomy, local colour, silhouette or object contact.

Beauty takes precedence over visible physical effect. If relief competes with the face, focal colour, silhouette, atmosphere or viewing path, reduce it.

Tactile depth cannot repair weak composition, unattractive facial construction, literal prop copying, unmotivated subject light, or dirty grading. Correct those modules first; then add mild body only to the spatial carriers that remain useful.

## Compact Prompt Clause

```text
Light tactile depth: the painting's depth is already established by composition, overlap, scale, colour and edge hierarchy. Keep most passages low-relief. Add slight loaded body only on [named overlap/contact/form turn/directional crest/foreground separator] where it clarifies the front-back relationship. Keep [named continuous, distant or identity-critical fields] broad, connected and comparatively flat. Let marks follow form and taper into surrounding paint; use only narrow subordinate ridge catches. Avoid equal-size knife patches, puzzle-piece tessellation, cracked-plaster texture, full-frame embossing, global varnish and relief that competes with the image.
```

Remove this clause entirely when tactile depth is not requested.

## Review Tests

- **Relief-removal test:** if all surface height vanished, would composition, colour, shape and spatial depth still work? If not, rebuild the image before adding body.
- **Depth-ownership test:** can every conspicuous raised passage be tied to a visible overlap, contact, turn, crest or foreground separation?
- **Continuity test:** do sky, skin, atmosphere and other broad fields remain connected instead of breaking into equal chips?
- **Distance test:** do farther and quieter passages carry less physical presence than foreground or focal carriers?
- **Mosaic test:** at normal viewing scale, do repeated similar-size rectangles, polygons or knife islands become a dominant pattern? If yes, fail.
- **Beauty test:** does the tactile effect improve form and depth without competing with face, colour, rhythm or atmosphere?

## Targeted Correction

When the result looks tiled or sculpted, do not reduce every tile uniformly. Remove the global height logic, reconnect the large colour/value shapes, and rebuild only the few spatial carriers that genuinely benefit from mild body. Keep the accepted composition, colour authorship and recognition anchors unchanged while correcting this module.
